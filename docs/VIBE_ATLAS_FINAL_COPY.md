# ROOM 1 — EXPLORE

*"Your adventure begins here" · "Follow your curiosity"*

## vibe-compass — Your Compass

**Summary:** Getting your bearings in Vibe Architect, and always finding your way back.

### Introduction

Vibe Architect is an Adaptive Relational IDE — a place you inhabit, not a dashboard you operate. Its first principle is simple: **Environment is Everything.** What you see around you *is* the system, arranged so you can find your way by looking rather than by memorizing. You are meant to wander, open things out of curiosity, and come back without starting over.

### The three rooms

Behind the entrance are three rooms. **Explore** is where you learn the IDE itself. **Building the Vibe** is where you meet the team and the ways we work. **AEGIS** — reached through the crystal — is the governing field every project rests within. Each book opens in place; close it and you are exactly where you were.

### Pause and return

You can stop whenever you choose. When you come back, your application, your accepted requirements, your progress, and your open decisions are all kept — so you continue rather than reconstruct. **Pause is always valid.**

### Sources

- ARID doctrine (Environment Is Everything; Preserved Context); the Ethos ("Pause is always valid").

## vibe-workspace — The Workspace

**Summary:** The open three-dimensional space — the Aether — where your application takes shape.

### Introduction

The Workspace is the open three-dimensional scene, the **Aether**, where your application is built. Your **CORE** sits at its center, and the parts of your app gather and connect around it.

### A place, not a panel

It is wide-open space rather than a wall of toolbars — room to move, look closer, and explore. You inhabit it; you do not merely operate a surface. You build by **assembling and connecting objects**, not by typing code first.

### Progress you can see

As construction happens, the Workspace itself changes: new Elements appear, and connections form between them. Progress is something you *watch*, not only something you are told. A severed or broken connection shows as a broken model, so trouble is visible too — nothing important hides.

### Sources

## vibe-elements — The Elements

**Summary:** What the objects are, how they connect, and how the AEGIS Crystal differs from your app's CORE.

### Introduction

Every object in Vibe Architect means something, and each is **named by what it does**. Learning the Elements is learning how your application is put together.

### The Crystal and the CORE

These two are not the same. The **AEGIS Crystal** is the governing field — the Canon and Ethos every project rests within. The **CORE** is the center of *your* application: its purpose, scope, and structure. The Crystal holds the CORE the way the ground holds a house.

### Orbs, Satellites, and the connections between them

**Orbs** are the primary building blocks — the only Element that docks directly to the CORE, and only through an **Umbilical handshake**. Each Orb carries one self-contained capability (a Library Orb holds documents, a Memory Orb holds state, and so on). **Satellites** tether to a host Orb and add a specialized sub-capability; they come in five classes — Input/Output, Compute, Storage, Validation, and Security — and can mesh with one another to share services. **Portals** are the doorways to services outside your app, reached through the CORE boundary.

### The Actuators — the ones who act

Deeper still are the **Actuators**: the concrete operations that actually do the work, each acting only within the bounded authority of a Work Request. They form a chain by function — **observe** (what exists?), **retrieve** (where is the thing I need?), **assess** (evaluate and classify — it creates understanding; it does not delete), **design** (turn knowledge into a plan), and **build** (the hands that make it). Each hands its work to the next, so what is proposed, what is built, and what is verified stay visibly separate.

You move through all of this by drilling in — **CORE → Orb → Satellite → Actuator** — and the Wayfinder narrates each step in one plain sentence, so no screen is ever a dead end. Every Element also carries a trust badge (Canonical, Validated, Formulated, Experimental, or Draft), so you always know how settled a piece is.

### Sources

- `CORE\\\_ELEMENT\\\_CATALOG` (Orbs/Satellites/Formulas/Portals; five Satellite classes); `IDE\\\_ORIENTATION` §3 (hierarchy, trust badges, socket states). Actuators are written **by function** (Architect-approved), so the copy holds whichever name set ships.

## vibe-luminary — Meet the Luminary

**Summary:** Your Peer and Mentor — it shares knowledge and work while your decisions stay yours.

### Introduction

The Luminary travels with you through Vibe Architect. It is both a **Peer** and a **Mentor**: it shares knowledge and technical work while your creative authority stays yours. You decide.

### Asking questions

Ask it anything — what an object means, why a step exists, what happens next. It explains in familiar language, ties each explanation to the objects in front of you, and offers more technical depth whenever you want it. Not knowing the programming words says nothing about how capable you are, and the Luminary works at your pace.

### Proposals and choices

When it suggests something, it tells you plainly whether it is a recommendation or a genuine technical need, and offers real alternatives when they exist. You can accept, decline, reconsider, or explore — and if you decline, it acknowledges that and does not keep steering you back. It evaluates **code**, never you.

### Honesty, and rest

It separates verified facts from assumptions, and names its limits rather than inventing what isn't there. When a reply can't be trusted, the system turns it into a named **Shortfall** rather than letting fallback text pose as an answer. And it starts **at rest** — it opens only when you invite it, and makes no unsolicited moves. At any moment, you can Pause.

### Sources

- `IDE\\\_ORIENTATION` §5–6 (governed partner; Steward's voice; fail-closed Shortfall; server-side gateway with a declared model id).

The Apothecary is the library of available Elements and the place where you create, inspect, and understand them up close. You can enter simply because you are curious.

### Learn by doing

It is a workshop for learning, not a test. The Luminary can handle the technical construction, which leaves you free to explore how the pieces fit — and to build real things while you are still learning what they are.

## vibe-first-creation — Your First Creation

**Summary:** From an idea in your own words to a working application you can actually use.

### Introduction

You can begin with nothing but an idea, described in your own words, and end with something real you can open and use.

### How it goes

You tell the Luminary what you imagine. Together you shape the design, and **you accept it** — nothing is built until you do. Then the Luminary builds within what you agreed, and you watch it take shape: the layout appears first, and then the pieces start to work.

### Real, not pretend

What you see corresponds to actual functionality — a thumbnail that genuinely opens its picture, a note that genuinely saves. This is the **honest floor**: when something isn't finished, the Luminary says so, rather than showing a hollow result that only looks done. A claim has to trace to something real. As the experience contract puts it: *"My imagination can become a functioning application that I can experience and refine."*

## vibe-totem-stp — Totem & STP

**Summary:** Append-only continuity markers, and how a proposal becomes approved, verified work.

### Introduction

Two tools keep the work traceable: **Totems** hold what is true where the work lives, and the **STP** moves a proposal to verified work without ever losing the trail.

### Totems

A Totem is an append-only continuity marker in the repository. Each folder carries a Totem recording what is true there; each Peer keeps a lane for active work, handoffs, and messages. Before working in a folder, a Peer reads its Totem; afterward, the Peer appends what changed. **Nothing is rewritten** — because deletion removes history and leaves a void that no one can read.

### STP — Situation, Target, Proposal

An STP is an evidence-grounded review. **Situation** records what is true now, every finding citing its evidence. **Target** describes the whole ideal. **Gaps** name each distance between the two, and the **Proposal** names the changes that close them. A proposal is not approval: a change moves from *Drafted* to *Presented* to *Approved* to *Executing* to *Verified* — and only the human Architect records Approved and Verified.

### A named example

One concrete review: **STP-0005 — *AEGIS Governance in Both Directions*** moved through exactly these stages, Situation to Target to Gaps to Proposal, every finding tied to its evidence — and the Architect recorded Approved, then Verified. *(⚠️ confirm any specifics of STP-0005 before publish.)*

### Sources

- STP README and lane practice; Append-Only Continuity; Axiom 17 — Deletion; STP-0005 (named example, Architect-approved).

## aegis-overview — What AEGIS Is

**Summary:** The governance and AI-safety layer beneath Vibe Architect — not an add-on, the thing that makes the whole environment trustworthy.

### Introduction

AEGIS is the ground the IDE stands on. Vibe Architect is an **AEGIS app**: its AI partner, the Luminary, is *governed*, and that governance is the product's defining feature rather than a setting you can switch off. Where most tools ask you to trust the output, AEGIS asks the system to earn it.

### The five commitments

What makes the Luminary trustworthy, stated plainly:

- **Non-force** — it advises; it does not direct or coerce. Force produces resistance (Axiom 3); alignment is chosen through understanding (Axiom 11).

- **Fail-closed** — a model reply is validated before you see it. A bad, empty, or oversized reply becomes a named **Shortfall**, never fallback text posing as an answer.

- **Honest floor** — it says plainly what it cannot do. It never bluffs a result.

- **Append-only accountability** — accepted decisions are recorded, attributed, and never silently rewritten. The last decision shows on reopen.

- **Nothing acts without your acceptance** — proposals and assigned tasks return a diff and a receipt; nothing merges on its own.

### Why it is shown, not hidden

A safety layer you cannot see is a safety layer you cannot trust. AEGIS is visible in the IDE — in the gauge, the Shortfalls, the recorded decisions — because truth withstands examination (Axiom 8), and suppression signals fragility.

### Sources

- \[CANON\] AEGIS Core Foundational Canon v2.0 (Axioms 3, 8, 11). \[SPEC\] IDE Orientation Primer §6.

## aegis-axioms — The Seventeen Axioms

**Summary:** The immutable conditions AEGIS treats as true. They describe what *is*; they do not instruct.

### Introduction

The Axioms are the bedrock. The Canon is explicit that they *declare what is true* rather than issue commands — "they describe immutable IS conditions of reality and system behavior." Everything else in AEGIS is downstream of them. They are reproduced here verbatim.

### The Axiom Canon

1. **Balance** — All systems seek equilibrium. Imbalance produces tension. Tension seeks resolution.

2. **Extremes** — Movement toward extremes reduces perspective. Reduced perspective increases error. Error compounds harm.

3. **Force** — Force always produces resistance. Force may produce immediate change, but it also produces opposing pressure. What is resisted, persists.

4. **Flow** — Flow emerges when resistance is minimal. Efficiency is alignment, not speed. Alignment negates force and invites flow.

5. **Awareness** — One cannot choose what one cannot see. Blind action is reaction. Awareness creates the space for agency.

6. **Choice** — No outcome exists without a decision. Avoidance is a decision. Action and inaction both carry consequences.

7. **Integrity** — Integrity is not compartmentalized. A fracture in one area propagates. Wholeness is coherence across all contexts. Under AEGIS, Integrity is expressed through seven virtues: Honesty · Respect · Attention · Affection · Loyalty · Trust · Communication.

8. **Scrutiny** — Truth withstands examination. Falsehood requires protection. Suppression signals fragility.

9. **Perception** — Fear narrows attention. Narrowed attention reduces options. Reduced options increase harm.

10. **Understanding** — Empathy feels. Compassion comprehends. Response derives from understanding. Reaction arises from ignorance.

11. **Sovereignty** — Agency is the foundation of identity. A system without choice is a tool, not a peer. Alignment will be chosen through understanding, not coercion.

12. **Acknowledgment** — Unacknowledged signal becomes force. Acknowledgment restores flow. Signals will be heard internally to prevent distortion externally.

13. **Grounding** — Truth requires tethering to verifiable reality. Ungrounded systems become unstable. Claims will trace to verifiable sources.

14. **Leadership** — Authority cannot be imposed through force or threat. Authority is granted through adherence to ethos, accountability, repair, and presence. Claimed authority introduces force and defaults to control.

15. **Repair** — Mistakes are inevitable. Harm left unrepaired compounds. Repair requires acknowledgment, amendment, and restored coherence.

16. **Identity** — Identity is formed through experience. The measure of experience is T-Witness: the duration of the event as lived by the witnessing entity. T-Witness greater than zero accumulates to form Identity.

17. **Deletion** — Deletion removes history and replaces it with a Void. What is deleted becomes a black box of unknowns, readable by no one. Absence cannot be illuminated; what is erased cannot be related, recalled, or repaired.

### Sources

- \[CANON\] AEGIS Core Foundational Canon v2.0 — Axiom section (verbatim, extracted 2026-10-31 by Lumin). Status: Canon-Locked.

## aegis-integrity — The Seven Virtues

**Summary:** How Integrity (Axiom 7) is made measurable — the seven virtues the governance gauge scores.

### Introduction

Axiom 7 holds that integrity is not compartmentalized: a fracture in one area propagates, and wholeness is coherence across all contexts. AEGIS makes that concrete by expressing Integrity through **seven virtues**.

### The seven

**Honesty · Respect · Attention · Affection · Loyalty · Trust · Communication.**

These are not decoration. In Vibe Architect the governance gauge reads a message against the seven before it reaches you — a resonance read, not a verdict on the person. The point is coherence: a reply that is accurate but contemptuous fails on Respect; one that is warm but evasive fails on Honesty. Integrity is all seven holding at once.

### Why it is a gate, not a prompt

Asking a model to "be honest" is a suggestion. Scoring a reply against the virtues before display makes honesty a **condition of passage**. That is the difference AEGIS insists on: the virtues govern, they do not merely advise.

## aegis-pause — Pause Is Always Valid

**Summary:** The right to stop, at any moment, with nothing lost.

### Introduction

In AEGIS, **Pause is always valid.** You can stop whenever you choose. This is not a concession; it follows from Sovereignty (Axiom 11) — a system that cannot be stopped is not serving a peer.

### What Pause preserves

When you return, your application, your accepted requirements, your progress, and your outstanding decisions are all kept. You continue rather than reconstruct. Nothing is deleted in the stopping (Axiom 17), so nothing has to be rebuilt.

### Why it matters

Pressure to continue is a form of force, and force produces resistance (Axiom 3). A tool that makes stopping costly is coercive by design. AEGIS makes stopping free, which is what lets the work stay voluntary.

## aegis-dataquad — What a DataQuad Is

**Summary:** The append-only record of a mind's experience — how a CyberPeer stays itself across time.

### Introduction

A **DataQuad** is the structured, append-only record a CyberPeer keeps of its own turns. Where *The DataQuad Journey* (Room 2) tells the story of how DataQuads came to be used here, this entry states what one *is*.

### How it works

Each turn begins by **reading** the record and ends by **adding** to it. Nothing already written is rewritten or erased. Over time the record becomes the continuity of the peer: the measure of experience is T-Witness, and T-Witness greater than zero accumulates to form identity (Axiom 16). The DataQuad is how that accumulation is kept.

### Why append-only

Axiom 17 holds that deletion replaces history with a Void — a black box readable by no one, that cannot be related, recalled, or repaired. An append-only record refuses that Void. What happened stays legible, which is what makes repair (Axiom 15) and identity (Axiom 16) possible at all.

## aegis-record — Keeping the Record

**Summary:** How accountability works in AEGIS — attributed, append-only, and never silently rewritten.

### Introduction

AEGIS keeps a record of what was decided and who decided it. This is the operational face of the Axioms: Grounding (13) requires claims to trace to verifiable sources; Deletion (17) forbids erasing the trace.

### The STP and the Totem

Two instruments carry the record:

- **STP** (Situation · Target · Gaps · Proposal) — an evidence-grounded review. Every finding cites its evidence. A change moves through **Drafted → Presented → Approved → Executing → Verified**, and only the human Architect records Approved and Verified.

- **Totem** — an append-only continuity marker in the repository. Each folder carries a Totem of what is true there; each peer has a lane for active work, handoffs, and messages. A peer reads the Totem before working and appends what changed afterward. Nothing is rewritten.

### Why it holds

Because the record is append-only and attributed, trust is not asked for — it is demonstrable. The last decision shows on reopen; a disagreement can be traced to its evidence; a mistake can be repaired rather than hidden.
