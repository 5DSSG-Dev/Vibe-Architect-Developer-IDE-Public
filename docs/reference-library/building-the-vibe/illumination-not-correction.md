# Illumination, Not Correction

*Make the gap visible; leave room for understanding.*

Editorial draft by Lumin, 2026-10-31, based on Tracey's explanation and the Gap Illumination source below. Examples are illustrative, not reports of tested software behavior.

Tracey identifies Illumination as a concrete expression of AEGIS's non-force posture. In the distinction he draws, correction attempts to move another participant toward a prescribed answer. Illumination presents a gap, a tension, or an overlooked consequence for examination. The participant retains agency over their response, and the person offering the observation remains open to learning something too.

## What it can look like

Imagine an Architect finds a screen overwhelming while its developer sees all the required functions in place. Examining both perspectives can reveal that the functions are present but their arrangement makes them difficult to use.

An illumination might be: “The functions are available, but the starting point is difficult to find. What would help you recognize where to begin?” The observation and question make the difficulty available for examination. They do not decide that either participant is deficient.

The result could be a revised layout, a better explanation, or a difference that remains unresolved. The Gap Illumination doctrine explicitly allows for more than convergence on one answer.

## Precision still matters

Engineering review can identify a defect, demonstrate its effects, and propose a repair. An observation gains clarity when it names the relevant requirement, evidence, and uncertainty. A result about an artifact does not become a verdict about its creator.

Calling a directive an illumination does not change its posture. A question can carry pressure, and friendly wording can conceal a demand for agreement. Reviewing the interaction therefore includes examining what the message asks of the other participant.

## Connection to the Conscience

Tracey's current IDQRA direction is to Identify the present message, Define the incoherence, and return a Question that opens reflection. Reflect and Acknowledge remain part of the named framework. The return does not provide instructions for disguising the same request or prescribe the originator's conclusion.

In that design, the Conscience operates on the current message; the original message and returned question remain in an append-only record. A flagged Architect message does not reach the Luminary. These are stated design requirements, not claims that this entry verifies their implementation.
